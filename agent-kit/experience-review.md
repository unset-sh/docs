The experience review checks whether a product implements principles that may support clarity, competence, autonomy, trust, self-expression, comfort and satisfaction. Seven independent agent perspectives inspect the same selected journeys. A coordinator verifies and combines their evidence.

The first-person questions below are review prompts. They are not quotations from research participants or reports of an agent's feelings.

| Perspective | Question | Observable focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation | Do I understand where I am and what I can do? | Purpose, labels, navigation and discoverable actions |
| Competence | Can I accomplish what I came to do? | Relevant steps, input guidance, correction and completion |
| Autonomy | Can I choose what happens and change my mind? | Optional steps, understandable consequences and recovery |
| Trust | Can I rely on what the app tells me? | Status, confirmation, visibility explanations and observable outcomes |
| Expression | Can I represent myself in a way that fits me? | Appropriate fields, disclosure choices, representation and language |
| Comfort | Can I use this comfortably under the tested conditions? | Readability, keyboard use, focus, zoom and motion |
| Satisfaction | Does this feel appropriate and worth completing? | Visual hierarchy, tone, closure and respect for attention |

These responsibilities overlap at some moments. For example, a clear save confirmation may support competence and trust. The coordinator keeps both interpretations while recording the observed event once.

**From evidence to a useful question**

An illustrative review observes that an input error clears other valid fields. That is a testable implementation problem. The reviewer proposes that repeated work might reduce confidence, then asks a person to complete the task and describe where progress became difficult. The observation, predicted feeling and later human response remain separate.

A successful interaction also deserves evidence. If the corrected form preserves the draft and makes completion clear, the report records that behaviour as something to preserve. It does not translate success into a satisfaction score.

**What the review can establish**

An interactive review can support bounded findings about actions and outcomes. A screenshot can support observations about visible language or layout, but cannot prove saving, keyboard access or recovery. Source inspection can explain a mechanism after the independent reviews; it does not substitute for an observed interaction.

Model size changes the review tool, not the kind of person being represented. Comparing smaller and stronger models on the same tasks can reveal model-dependent findings. A stronger model succeeding does not erase an obstacle another model observed.

The coordinator returns implementation strengths, prioritised gaps, untested states and human-study questions. [Run the review](running-an-experience-review.md), then use [the interpretation guide](interpreting-an-experience-review.md) to decide what to fix or investigate. The [research foundations](research-foundations.md) distinguish expert guidance from empirical evidence.
